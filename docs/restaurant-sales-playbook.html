The whole plan in one line: call in the morning, walk in the afternoon, follow up at night. Every restaurant gets the same sequence of touches until it buys or says no.

1. Is restaurants the right lane? Yes.

  • Big market: roughly 1 million restaurant locations in the US and about 19,000 in Georgia (National Restaurant Association estimates). You will not run out of doors.
  • You can reach them: they're in a building all day. You can walk into 15–20 in one afternoon.
  • Bigger card orders: a barber needs 1 card. A restaurant needs one per table, so 5–10+ per sale.
  • Repeat work: menu changes, price updates, specials and DoorDash upkeep can become a monthly fee.
  • Real proof: Exclusive Restaurant & Lounge in Covington, GA: TV menus plus review cards.

5. Scripts

Cold call (9:30–10:45)

"Hey, quick question. Who handles your menu boards and your DoorDash?" → "Got it, and when is [he/she] usually in?" → "Perfect. I'm Christopher with CEO Turbo. I just put the TV menus up at Exclusive Restaurant & Lounge in Covington. I'll be on your side of town today. Can I swing by at 3 for two minutes to show [name]?" → "And can I text you a photo of the Exclusive menu so [name] knows who I am?" ← this is your permission to text. Write down the yes.

The goal of the call is the stop-by, not the sale. Keep it under 60 seconds.

Permission text (only after they said yes)

Hey [name], it's Christopher from CEO Turbo, like I mentioned. Here's the menu I put on Exclusive's TVs in Covington: [photo] Full breakdown: https://www.ceoturbo.com/restaurants?utm_source=sms&utm_medium=permission See you around 3. Reply STOP to opt out.

Walk-in (about 90 seconds)

  1. Use their name: "Is [name] in? I called this morning. I'm Christopher."
  2. Show their own listing on your phone: "You're at 4.3 with 87 reviews. [Competitor] down the street has 4.6 and 412. When someone searches 'food near me,' they get picked first."
  3. Proof: the Exclusive menu photo, then 10 seconds of the video.
  4. Let them tap: hand over a card. "Tap your phone on this." Their own hands do the convincing.
  5. The offer: "A card on every table. Guests tap after they eat, while they're still happy. For 10 tables it's about $160, and I'll set them all up right now. If you want the menu on your TVs too, I can do that this week."
  6. The close: "Want me to set them up while I'm here?"

If the owner isn't in: leave a card and a flyer, ask "When is [name] usually in?", and come back then.

8. Why this works (the psychology)

  • Reciprocity: you call first, show up in person, and set things up for them.
  • Social proof: Exclusive, and then every restaurant after it.
  • Loss aversion: "reviews walking out the door," and their rating next to a competitor's.
  • Commitment: small yeses first: "Can I swing by?" "Can I text you the photo?" "Tap this card."
  • Names and specifics: their name, their rating, their real competitor. Specifics build trust.
